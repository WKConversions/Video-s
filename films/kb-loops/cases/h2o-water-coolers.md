# H2O Water Coolers: K.B case loop

Source: https://kruslockbosconsultancy.com/client-cases/h2o-water-coolers (fetched 12 Nov 2026). Only these facts go on
screen.

## Facts (case page)
- **Title:** "One inbox for every customer, AI-captured orders and daily route optimisation". H2O Water Coolers, an FMCG
  SME in Spain that delivers water to offices, moved every customer conversation into one support platform and automated
  order capture and daily route planning.
- **Challenge:** orders reached H2O through WhatsApp and other channels and were entered into the system by hand.
  Customer communication and order data were spread across fragmented tools, so orders were delayed, chances were missed
  and revenue was lost.
- **K.B's approach:** one support platform for all customer conversations, an AI agent that captures orders into the CRM,
  and a daily routing engine. All five channels (WhatsApp, website widget, email, Messenger and Instagram) feed into
  Chatwoot, with replies sent back through the same channel. Routes use only orders registered before each day's cutoff;
  every address has a delivery group and a map pin; agent-created orders are reviewed before the cutoff.
- **Result:** ~2 hours time saved per day on order handling and route planning, compared with manual entry.
- **Built with:** Chatwoot, Java Spring Boot, React.js.

## Loop
Concept: "Downhill to the door". A diagonal cascade from top-left to bottom-right, with the two free corners for words
and the result: five channel tiles (WhatsApp, website widget, email, Messenger, Instagram) bob in the top-left; in the
centre a grey order sheet where a caret types one slow order, a red clock ticking in its header; in the bottom-right a
Costa del Sol route map (sky-blue sea with H2O's wave edge, H2O depot, van marker, five empty pins) whose route is a grey
string tangled round the pins; in the top-right an H2O site card (logo, headline bars, blue pill, the cooler
illustration). Messages pop out of the channels and pile up crooked beside the sheet; one bounces off the pile, turns
red and drops away (a missed order). The K.B tile lands on the sheet's corner and the sheet turns into a Chatwoot inbox:
the five channel tiles fly down into its rail, the piled messages stretch into inbox rows, and the missed channel sends
its message again. A cyan AI scan line runs down the rows and stamps each one with a 19 L bottle chip and a check; the
chips hop down-right onto the map pins, which fill H2O blue. The Spring Boot tile joins the map, the route string
twangs taut, and the van marker runs the loop, ticking each pin. "~2 hours / saved per day" takes over the H2O card.
Then the string goes slack, pins empty, rows pop out, the channels fly home, the inbox turns back into the sheet and the
K.B tile lifts off: the opening.

Flourish: "the twang". The hand-planned route is a slack grey string snagged round the pins. When the orders land it
is plucked: it snaps taut into the clean delivery loop, overshoots, and every span between two pins vibrates like a
plucked guitar string while cyan after-images of its in-between shapes smear from the tangle into the loop and fade
(the tangle visibly smears into the route). Then white dashes flow round the loop while the van marker drives it.

Layout: channels top-left (x 184–756, y 110–250); message pile left of the centre card (x 460–640); order sheet / inbox
in the centre (x 660–1170, y 300–700) with the K.B tile on its top-right corner; route map bottom-right (x 1220–1764,
y 540–968); H2O card top-right (x 1250–1760, y 116–476), which carries the figure; text bottom-left (x 152, y 776–950).
Different from Vino Mio (wide floor-plan card filled by a liquid pour), Social Impact Factory (a smear that becomes a
cable) and the others: here the central idea is convergence then distribution down a diagonal, and the flourish is a
plucked string, not a liquid, comet, wipe or blob.
