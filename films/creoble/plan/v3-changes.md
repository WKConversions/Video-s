# Creoble v3: targeted changes to v2

## The client's own words (they override everything else, including the website)
> "I'd prefer to lean more towards a cream/off-white background, with light blue (Creoble's brand colour) as the
> primary colour and the cream tone as a secondary colour. (no orange) We'd also like to go for more of that clean,
> minimalist Apple-like aesthetic."

The client also shared a reference film (a LinkedIn branding agency's explainer) and said he liked its storytelling,
sound effects, animations and overall flow. Our producer's instruction: **don't replicate it.** Understand what he
liked and keep the style he described: Apple, minimalistic. "Really like Apple style." The blue (#4AA3F5, with
#2A86E0 for text and the cursor) is approved.

## What the reference does well (principles to keep, never its look)
- **One guide character drives the story:** a cursor (filled in the brand colour, white outline) that clicks, types,
  opens and pulls things in. Every scene change is caused by an action.
- **Calm, light ground:** one thing at a time, real UI shown big, push-ins on details.
- **Type alone on the ground:** a few words, built word by word, between product shots.
- **Before and after, and numbers that count.**
- **Clearly audible sound effects on every action:** clicks, keys, pops, swishes (about 1.6 a second).

## What v2 got wrong against the client's words (the fixes)
1. **Full-frame blue floods** (23.0–26.0 s, at "499" and at the start of hosting) break "cream background". In v3 the
   ground is cream everywhere. Blue lives in type accents, UI, soft blooms and the cursor.
2. **The opening clutter is busy** and has a black code card. It is not minimal.
3. **"No coding / No complexity" is busy:** a wall of grey code and four dense technical cards.
4. **Orange:** the bakery photos are now desaturated in the shared kit. Keep any warm colour out of everything else.
5. **The guide cursor:** the shared `Cursor` is now blue (#2A86E0) with a white outline. Use it as the film's guide
   wherever a person would act.

Everything else in v2 stays: the voice timings, the hand-off contracts (except the blue field, see scenes D and E),
the word builds, the 3D UI, the layer stack, the edit, the preview cards, the phone checkout, the globe, the hero and
the wordmark.
Keep motion alive, but calm and premium: Apple product-film restraint, generous whitespace, at most about 3–4 things
on screen.
