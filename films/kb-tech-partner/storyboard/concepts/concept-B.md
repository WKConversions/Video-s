# Concept B: People Behind the Tile

## 1. Thesis

**Thesis:** Technology is a wall of opaque tool tiles; K.B is the one tile that opens onto real people and real
work, proven with the real team photos and one real case (Göteborgsvarvet) acted out from first look to live result.

**Allowed patterns:** the site's real tool tiles as "technology" (stairs, sliding puzzle, escalator); one Mixkit clip
standing for the viewer's business, never shown as a client or the team; a speech bubble for "advice"; a cyan lens
for "understand"; a rebuilt spreadsheet standing for Göteborgsvarvet's Excel files, using only the case page's names
(Travel, Food, Materials, Logistics; Suppliers, Sponsors, Partners, Event staff) and no figures; the label "A real
K.B case"; the real dashboard screenshot; the figure "100% successful submissions"; a four-frame strip of the case.

## 2. Art direction

- **World.** The canvas is #F7F7F7 throughout. Windows are white, with 32 px corners and a soft navy shadow.
  - Navy #2A3B54 is used for tiles and type.
  - Cyan #38C6F4 is used only as marks (route, underline, lens, scan band, strip line), never as text, because the
    contrast on light grey is too low.
  - **One full navy field**, which the K.B tile grows into on "comes in" (10.45–11.3 s).
- **Type.** Montserrat ExtraBold 88–120 px for headlines, SemiBold 36–40 px for labels. "K.B" is always set in
  Copperplate Heavy. The film never shows a URL, "Consultancy", "Consultant" or an email address.
- **Media, decided once.**
  - Real photos: four team portraits, the Málaga founders photo, and the Göteborgsvarvet runners photo.
  - The real Göteborgsvarvet dashboard, shown at 1.3× so it reads.
  - The real tool logos on navy tiles.
  - One Mixkit clip of a small team at work: bright, people moving, no logos. It stands for "your business"
    because the script addresses the viewer. It is also the only material that moves on its own (see 4).
- **Thread: the rounded tile.**
  - Setup: opaque tool tiles carry the business, then box it in.
  - Turn: the K.B tile, the same shape, fills the one gap.
  - Development: K.B tiles are *windows*. They open onto the four team faces, then onto the case.
  - Callback: the tool tiles return as an escalator going nowhere.
  - Payoff: the founders rise *behind* the business, the escalator turns upward, and the team window closes into
    the end-card tile.
- **Motion personality: "conveyor and settle".**
  - Working layers travel at a constant speed (B10, 40–150 px/s).
  - Arrivals that matter are long (B3, 0.5–0.7 s, no overshoot), and exits accelerate (A1 halves).
  - Entrance pattern: everything is carried in on a conveyor, or rises inside its own frame from a masked edge.
    Nothing pops from zero and nothing simply fades up.
  - The camera only breathes (1.02 ± 1.5% over about 20 s) and comes to rest by 33 s.
- **Transitions.** There are three behaviours:
  1. Layout transformation: tiles become stairs, then a wall, a puzzle, an escalator; the bubble becomes portraits;
     cells become dashboard cards; the case becomes a strip.
  2. A mask out of an object: the K.B tile grows into the field, the lens grows into the window, the team window
     closes into the logo.
  3. A persistent window with new content rising inside it.

## 4. Reaching 5% without the camera or a busy frame

I measured with motion_check's own method (1920→160×90 INTER_LINEAR, change above 6 levels) on synthetic frames
built from our real assets (`scratchpad/mtest*.py`):

| Content | Share of the frame changing per frame |
|---|---|
| Runners photo, 1240×760 window, 70 / 40 px/s | **25% / 19%** (pale 14%) |
| Runners photo, 800×500 window, 30 px/s | 7.7% |
| Four portraits, 40 px/s | 5.0% |
| Group photo, 1100×700, 40 px/s vertical | 5.7% |
| Bold sheet scrolling, 120 / 60 px/s | 10% / 4.1% |
| Navy field growing | 7.8% |
| Tile conveyor, one row / two rows | 2.0% / 4.1% |
| One tile sliding | 1.5% |
| Dashboard UI scrolling | 2–2.4% |
| Type line moving | 0.5% |

Type, tiles and UI barely register; **real photographs drifting inside windows that hold still carry the number**.
So in concept B the proof is also the motion engine.

**Build rule.** Every phrase except the logo hold and the end card keeps a photo or footage layer drifting at
40–80 px/s. Each drift eases in over at least 0.5 s and is never reversed on a beat. These drifts are elements
moving, not the camera, they stay under the probe's 300 px/s FLYING limit, and there are never more than three
motion groups at once.

**Per beat:**
- Hook: about 6–10%, but only if the clip changes at least 15% inside its own frame.
- Problem: 5–7%.
- 07: above 8%, then about 1% during the logo hold.
- 08–09: about 5%.
- Case, 10–12: 14–25%.
- 13: about 8%.
- 14: 6%, falling to 2.5%.
- 15–18: about 10%.
- 19–21: 8–12%.

**Predicted median: 7–9%.**

**Riskiest stretches**, about 5.6 s in total (16% of the film):

| Time | Stretch | Level | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1.1 s | tiles only | ~3% | Add a second row |
| 8.4–9.3 s | boxed in | ~4% | Slide tiles in pairs |
| 10.6–11.3 s | logo hold | ~1% | Accepted |
| 12.3–13.0 s | bubble | ~5% | Depends on the clip |
| 20.5–21.1 s | dashboard | ~2.5% | Start 15's move early |
| 32.6–34 s | end card | ~1% | Calm by Karl's rule |

## 6. Risks, cuts, assets

**Risks.**
- **The clip.** If no Mixkit candidate changes at least 15% inside its frame, the hook and the payoff drop to about
  3%.

  the buttons and the table's source names.
- **Clearance.** The Göteborgsvarvet name, photo and screenshot need clearing with K.B (missing.md §6).

  10 with the probe.
