# Double VANOS on the BMW M54B30 (2001 E46 330Ci coupe)

Topic folder: `vanos`. Images: `/home/user/Video-s/films/bmw-e46/assets/ref/vanos/`.
The car (RDW): 330Ci coupe, M54B30, 2979 cc, 170 kW, first registered 9 Apr 2001, automatic (per owner).
VANOS is part of the engine. It is the same with the manual or the automatic gearbox.
On the 2001 car the engine computer is the Siemens MS43 [S2][S4].

## Plain-Dutch summary (for the voice-over and captions)

VANOS staat voor "VAriable NOckenwellenSteuerung": variabele nokkenasverstelling.
Op de 330Ci verstelt het systeem beide nokkenassen: inlaat en uitlaat. Daarom heet het "dubbel VANOS".
Een aluminium blok voor op de cilinderkop gebruikt motorolie om de nokkenassen een paar graden te verdraaien.
Zo gaan de kleppen eerder of later open: meer trekkracht bij lage toeren, vol vermogen bij hoge toeren, en een rustiger stationair toerental.

### How it works

| id | fact (EN) | NL (on-screen) | value | conf | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| oil-driven | VANOS is moved by ordinary engine oil pressure from the engine's oil pump. Oil flows from the pump to the solenoid valves and drains back to the sump. Unlike the M3 engines (S50B32, S54), the M54 has no separate high-pressure VANOS pump. | VANOS werkt op de gewone motoroliedruk. | engine oil pressure | H | [S1] p.13, [S8] (high-pressure pump only on the M engines) |
| solenoids | Two solenoid valves (one for intake, one for exhaust), which BMW calls "three-way solenoid switching valves", steer the oil. The engine computer (MS43) switches them with pulse-width modulation at about 100-220 Hz, so a piston can be held in any position. | De motorcomputer stuurt twee magneetkleppen aan. | 100-220 Hz | H | [S2] p.32-33, [S4] p.114, [S1] p.14 |
| helical | Oil pressure pushes a piston. The piston moves a cup with slanted (helical) teeth inside and out. The cup links the camshaft to its chain sprocket. Because the teeth are slanted, pushing the cup in or out twists the camshaft a few degrees against the sprocket. The valves then open earlier (advance) or later (retard). | Olie duwt een zuiger. Schuine tandjes zetten dat duwen om in draaien. | helical (slanted) splines | H | [S2] p.32 ("angled teeth ... converted into a rotational movement"), [S4] p.112, [S13] |
| control-inputs | The computer sets the cam positions from engine speed and throttle position, then corrects for intake-air and coolant temperature. It checks the actual positions with the cam sensors. | De computer kijkt naar toerental, gaspedaal en temperatuur. | - | H | [S2] p.33, [S4] p.113 |
| fully-variable | Both camshafts are "fully" (continuously) variable within the mechanical travel. Once the target is reached, oil pressure on both sides of each piston holds the position. | Traploos verstelbaar, niet alleen aan of uit. | continuous | H | [S2] p.33, [S1] p.13-14, [S6] |
| range-intake | Intake camshaft adjustment range: 40° of crankshaft rotation. The lobe centre moves from 120° to 80° after TDC. That is 20° of camshaft rotation. | Inlaatnokkenas: 40 graden verstelbereik. | 40° crank (80-120° ATDC) | H | [S2] p.34 diagram, [S1] p.19 diagram, [S5] |

### What the driver gets

| id | fact (EN) | NL (on-screen) | value | conf | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| torque | More torque in the low and middle rev range (BMW names 1500-2000 rpm), without losing power at high revs. | Meer trekkracht bij lage toeren. | 1500-2000 rpm | H | [S2] p.32, [S3] p.27, [S1] p.12 |
| power | At high revs the valves are timed for maximum filling, so full power is kept. | Vol vermogen bij hoge toeren. | - | H | [S6], [S1] p.12 |
| idle | Smoother idle: less valve overlap at idle, so less burnt gas stays in the cylinder. | Rustiger stationair toerental. | - | H | [S2] p.32, [S1] p.12, [S10] p.8 |
| egr | At part load more overlap gives "internal exhaust gas recirculation": a little exhaust gas is drawn back in. This lowers combustion temperature and NOx without a separate EGR valve. | Minder uitstoot: een beetje uitlaatgas gaat opnieuw de cilinder in. | internal EGR | H | [S2] p.35, [S4] p.115, [S1] p.12 |

### Common failure (background only)

| id | fact (EN) | NL | conf | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| seals | The piston seals are PTFE rings over nitrile (Buna-N/NBR) O-rings. The O-rings harden and shrink with age and heat. Oil then leaks past the pistons and VANOS cannot hold the cam position. Typical result: less low-rev torque (below about 3000 rpm), hesitation, rougher idle, higher fuel use, sometimes an exhaust-VANOS fault code. BMW sells only the complete unit. Aftermarket Viton seal kits are common. | Bekende kwaal: de rubber O-ringen in de zuigers worden hard. | M | [S13], [S8] (problems after about 100,000 km), [S5] |
| rattle | Wear in the helical gears and axial play in the piston bearing can cause a rattle, reported at about 1800-2200 rpm. | Gerammel door slijtage van de schuine tandjes. | M | [S17], [S13] (rattle page) |

## Modelling notes

### Dimensions

All dimensions below are ESTIMATES. They are scaled from repair photos ([S13], [I-F1]) using the 10 mm flange nuts and an assumed camshaft centre distance of about 125 mm. No BMW drawing with dimensions was found. Allow about ±15 %.

**VANOS housing.** Cast aluminium, about 265 mm wide (across the engine) x 150 mm high x 70 mm deep (front to back, without the solenoids).

- **Front face shape.** Seen from the front it looks like a dog-bone or a pair of spectacles: two round bosses joined by a slanted, ribbed bridge.
- **Bosses.** Each boss is about 90 mm across and stands about 20 mm proud of the bridge. Each is centred on a camshaft axis. Boss centres are about 125 mm apart.
- **Piston caps.** In the middle of each boss is a round cap about 35 mm across, with a small central hole or hex.
- **Ribs.** The bridge has 3-4 diagonal ribs and a small raised rectangular casting mark.
- **Bottom flange.** A thicker bar (about 25 mm) runs along the bottom edge and carries the oil passages. A thin flange lip follows the outline of the head's front face, with 6 nuts along the lower half and 1 bolt at the top corner.

**Solenoids.**

- **Exhaust solenoid.** A silver (zinc-plated) cylinder about 30 mm across x 120 mm long. It lies horizontally along the lower edge, below the exhaust boss, and has a black plastic 2-pin connector at its outer end.
- **Intake solenoid.** A similar silver cylinder about 28 x 90 mm with a black connector. It sits at the intake end of the housing, next to the oil-feed banjo bolt (19 mm) and an engine lifting eye.

**Oil feed.** A thin steel pipe (about 10 mm) runs from the oil-filter housing on the left side of the engine to the intake end of the VANOS.

**Behind the housing** (hidden until the VANOS is pulled away):

- **Sprockets.** Two steel sprockets, each about 100 mm across and about 10 mm thick, on the cam axes. The exhaust sprocket is a double sprocket.
- **Chains.** Dark-steel single-row roller chains, about 10 mm wide. One chain runs over both sprockets. The primary chain runs from the exhaust sprocket down to the crankshaft.
- **Camshaft noses.** Each camshaft nose has a short splined stub (about 30 mm across) that the helical cup slides over.

**For the explainer cut-away.**

- **Cylinder.** One bore about 60 mm across x 50 mm deep.
- **Piston.** A stepped piston: large end about 55 mm across, small end about 40 mm.
- **Helical cup.** A short tube about 45 mm across x 40 mm long, with slanted teeth outside and inside.
- **Sprocket and camshaft.** The sprocket has slanted internal teeth, and the camshaft nose has slanted external teeth.
- **Animation.**
  - Move the piston and cup about 10 mm axially.
  - Rotate the camshaft (not the sprocket) by up to 20° (intake) or 12.5° (exhaust).
  - Add a small coil spring behind the exhaust piston.

### Colours and materials

- **Housing.** Cast aluminium, satin silver-grey, about #B9B7AE. The real inner surfaces turn gold-brown with oil varnish (#B08A3E). Keep the model clean silver.
- **Solenoids.** Bright zinc, about #C8C8C8.
- **Connectors.** Black plastic, about #151515.
- **Sprockets.** Steel grey, about #6E6E6E.
- **Chain.** Dark steel, about #3C3C3C.
- **Highlight.** Use the film's accent colour as an emissive rim or outline on the housing only. Keep the solenoids slightly darker so the two round bosses read first.

### Position in the car

The model uses the same axes as the engine-head notes:

- **X:** forward from the front-axle centre line.
- **Y:** to the left of the car's centre line.
- **Z:** up from the ground.

**Lengthwise.** The VANOS is at the very front of the engine, on top: directly in front of the cylinder-head front face and above the timing case and water pump. It sits behind the fan and radiator, under the front edge of the valve cover.

- **Head front face:** about X = +0.33 to +0.37 m. This assumes cylinders 3/4 sit over the axle, as measured in the engine-head research; cylinder 1 is then at about +0.23 m.
- **VANOS front face:** about X = +0.40 to +0.44 m.
- **Uncertainty:** these figures inherit the engine-head estimate error of about ±80 mm.

**Height and side.** Use the engine-local upright frame (Z measured from the crank axis, which is about 370 mm above the ground):

- **Cam axes:** about 320 mm above the crank axis (estimate: deck about 215 mm plus about 105 mm).
- **Intake cam:** 62 mm left of the engine centre plane.
- **Exhaust cam:** 62 mm right of the engine centre plane.

**After the engine tilt.** Apply the engine tilt from the engine-head notes: about 30° to the right, top towards the right, low confidence.

| | Y (left of centre line) | Z (above ground) |
|---|---|---|
| Intake cam / intake boss | about -105 mm (right of the centre line) | about 680 mm |
| Exhaust cam / exhaust boss | about -215 mm | about 615 mm |

The line between the bosses therefore slopes down towards the car's right at the tilt angle. The whole VANOS face is turned with the head.

**Orientation.** The front face points forward (+X). The piston axes are parallel to the crankshaft.

**Pull-out move.** Slide the unit straight forward (+X) about 150-200 mm along the camshaft axes (the real removal direction). Then untilt it by 30° and turn it to face the camera. Optionally leave the two sprockets and chain behind, glowing, to show where it fits.

### Recognisable features

- The two round bosses with centre caps, joined by a ribbed bridge.
- The silver solenoid cylinders with black plugs.
- The chain and sprockets just behind it.
- On the real car it sits right behind the fan, under the front of the black valve cover. From the front only the lower part of the face shows.

## Open questions

- Minimum oil pressure for VANOS: no BMW figure found (only owner reports and the 2-8 bar compressed-air workshop check).
- Year of the first double VANOS: 1995 (BMW Group Classic, de.wikipedia) vs 1996 (en.wikipedia) vs 1997 (BMW Lexikon). 1995 is used.
- Exact VANOS housing dimensions and cam centre distance: estimated from photos only.
- Exact orientation of the intake solenoid (sideways vs forward): photos are ambiguous. It is at the intake end.
- Cam duration for the M54B30: 228° on BMW's drawing vs 240/244° on forums. Not needed for the VANOS story.
- Engine tilt (about 30°) and lengthwise position come from the engine-head research and carry its uncertainty.
- Whether the brother's car has had the VANOS seals redone is unknown (not needed for the film).
