Common questions
If you want to know how to identify anonymous website visitors in B2B, the honest answer is that you match a slice of your traffic to a company or a person, then treat that match as a signal to act on, not as a confirmed buyer. Most of your traffic will stay anonymous. That is fine. The point is not to unmask everyone. The point is to catch the accounts that are quietly researching you and reach them before they fill out a form or, more likely, before they never do.
Website visitor identification has become a standard layer in the 2026 GTM stack for turning anonymous traffic into signals (reachrobin / heyreach, 2026). I run this play for clients, and I want to walk you through what it actually does, what it cannot do, and how to wire a visit into a real outreach motion without lying to yourself about match rates or stepping on privacy law.
What "identifying" a visitor actually means
There are two very different things people mean when they say visitor ID, and conflating them is where most teams get burned.
Company-level identification takes the IP address of a visitor and maps it back to an organisation using reverse IP and firmographic databases. You learn that someone at Acme Corp visited your pricing page three times this week. You do not learn who. This works across most geographies and is the safer, more durable layer.
Person-level identification goes further and resolves an individual: a name, a work email, sometimes a LinkedIn profile. This is what tools like RB2B built their reputation on. It relies on identity graphs stitched together from cookie pools, form fills, and data partnerships. It is powerful and it is far more constrained than the marketing suggests.
Here is the constraint nobody puts on the landing page: person-level match is essentially a US-only capability. RB2B and similar tools resolve individuals in the United States because that is where the consented identity data exists at scale. In the EU and UK, GDPR and ePrivacy make silently identifying a named individual from an anonymous page view a legal problem, not a feature. So if your buyers are in Europe, plan to run company-level ID plus enrichment, not person-level unmasking.
The match-rate reality (read this before you buy)
Every vendor demo shows a dashboard full of resolved contacts. Your real numbers will be lower, and that is normal.
I am not going to quote you a magic percentage, because the honest figure depends entirely on your traffic mix: geography, whether visitors are on corporate networks or mobile, how much of your audience the vendor's identity graph actually covers. Anyone who promises you a fixed match rate across all your traffic is selling, not measuring. Run a two-week test on your own site with your own traffic before you believe any number.
My rule of thumb: treat company-level ID as your reliable base layer and person-level ID as a bonus that shows up for a fraction of US sessions. Build your process so it still works if the person-level match never fires.
The mistake I see over and over is teams buying a visitor-ID tool expecting a full contact list, then abandoning it in a month because the "list" is a trickle. The trickle is the product. A few high-intent accounts a week, worked properly, beats a thousand cold names.
From a visit to a trigger: the part that makes money
Identification is worthless on its own. A resolved company sitting in a dashboard is not a pipeline. The value comes from what you do in the next few minutes.
Visitor ID feeds signal-based selling: a visit triggers enrichment and a personalised touch instead of waiting for a form fill (Factors.ai, 2026). That sentence contains the whole play, so let me unpack it into steps.
1. Capture the raw signal. A known company (or person) hits a meaningful page. Not your blog. Your pricing page, your comparison page, your product pages. Weight the pages so a careers-page visit does not fire the same alarm as three visits to pricing in one day.
2. Enrich immediately. A company name is not enough to act on. You need the right contacts, their roles, company size, tech stack, and a work email you can actually reach. This is where a data enrichment waterfall earns its keep: you run the identified account through several providers in sequence so you get the highest-quality match without paying for every provider on every lookup.
3. Qualify against your ICP. Kill the noise here. Competitors, students, existing customers, and companies far outside your fit should be filtered before a human or a sequence ever sees them. Most identified visitors are not buyers. Your job is to find the few that are.
4. Trigger the touch. Now you reach out with context. Not "I saw you on our website," which is creepy and lazy. Instead, a relevant, timely message tied to the problem that page solves. The visit tells you what they care about right now. Use that to be useful, not to show off that you tracked them.
Why the trigger beats the blast
The reason this whole motion is worth building is timing. You are contacting someone during active research instead of interrupting them cold.
The numbers back this up. Signal-triggered outreach reply rates of 15 to 25 percent far exceed generic cold email at 3.43 percent (Factors.ai, 2026). That gap is not about clever copy. It is about relevance and timing. A message that lands while someone is comparing you to a competitor is a different animal from the same message sent to a name pulled off a list for no reason.
This is also why I push clients toward company-level ID plus strong enrichment rather than obsessing over person-level match. You do not need to know exactly which person browsed. You need to know the account is in-market, then reach the right people there with a reason to talk.
The privacy and consent reality
I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice, but you cannot run this play responsibly without a few non-negotiables.
Person-level identification of individuals in the EU and UK without a lawful basis is not a grey area, it is a risk you should not take. Keep person-level resolution to US traffic where the identity data is consented.
Your cookie banner and privacy policy have to reflect what you actually do. If you deploy a pixel that participates in an identity graph, disclose it. Honour consent choices and do-not-sell signals. And apply basic decency in the outreach: reference the problem, not the surveillance. "I noticed you were on our site at 2:14pm" destroys trust. "Teams evaluating X usually run into Y, is that on your radar" respects it.
Being honest here is not just ethics, it is durability. The teams that treat visitor ID as a stalking tool get flagged, blocked, and complained about. The teams that treat it as an early-warning system build a channel that keeps working.
How to actually stand this up
Keep it small at first. Pick your three highest-intent pages. Deploy one company-level tool and, if your buyers are US-based, layer a person-level tool on top. Wire identified accounts into an enrichment step, filter hard against your ICP, and route the survivors into a lightweight sequence or straight to a rep for the hottest ones. Measure reply rate and meetings, not resolved-contact counts. The vanity number is how many visitors you identified. The real number is how many turned into conversations.

Common questions
Can you identify every anonymous website visitor?
No, and you should not build your process assuming you can. You will resolve a subset to the company level and a smaller subset, mostly US traffic, to the person level. The majority of your visitors stay anonymous. The goal is to catch the in-market accounts, not to unmask everyone.
Is identifying anonymous visitors legal under GDPR?
Company-level identification via reverse IP is generally lower risk. Person-level identification of named individuals in the EU and UK without a lawful basis under GDPR and ePrivacy is a real legal problem, which is why most person-level tools operate on US traffic. Disclose what you do, honour consent, and get proper advice for your situation.
RB2B vs Vector vs Warmly, which should I use?
They overlap more than they differ: pixel-based visitor identification feeding a signal workflow. Person-level resolution skews US. Rather than pick on brand, run a two-week test on your own traffic and judge each on real match quality for your geography and audience, not the demo dashboard.
What do I do the moment a visitor is identified?
Enrich the account, qualify it against your ICP, then trigger a relevant, timely touch tied to the page they viewed. The identification is just the starting gun. The enrichment and the outreach are what create pipeline.
