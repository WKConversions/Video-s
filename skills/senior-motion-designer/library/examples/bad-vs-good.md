# Bad vs good decisions

Examples of the principles at work, including approved solutions from Karl's projects. They show the
reasoning; never reuse the scenes themselves in another film. Every stronger version shows the
meaning with the real things (`planning/visual-strategy.md`).

## "Everything you need, in one place."

Weak: five unrelated icons fly in from different sides with a bounce.
Stronger: the client's scattered tools (their real app windows, rebuilt and named) sit in separate
corners of the frame; one spatial action draws them into the product's single screen, which settles;
the headline appears only once the consolidation is clear.
Why: the motion itself communicates consolidation, with things the viewer recognizes.

## "Move faster."

Weak: the words MOVE FASTER shaking.
Stronger: the old way appears as several slow, sequential steps (the real screens or forms); then the
distance between them collapses into one continuous movement in the product, and the same outcome
arrives in a fraction of the space and time.
Why: the viewer experiences the reduction in friction.

## "See what matters."

Weak: an eye icon with a sparkle.
Stronger: the client's real, dense information (a busy report, a full inbox) at low contrast; depth,
focus and contrast isolate the one row that matters while the rest recedes, and the camera pushes in
until it reads.
Why: hierarchy performs the meaning.

## A UI feature

Weak: a full dashboard screenshot scales from 90% to 100% and a cursor clicks one button.
Stronger: establish enough dashboard for context, shift focus to the relevant card while the rest
recedes, and let the card become the spatial bridge into the detailed feature view.
Why: the animation controls attention instead of asking the viewer to search the interface.

## The September 2026 WKConversions ad (voice-over → picture that worked)

- "We find the one thing that matters…" → a highlight hops across candidate phrases in a dense
  paragraph and lands on the one that matters; the other words clear away.
- "…and build the motion around it." → a video frame grows around that phrase and starts playing,
  while an animation timeline underneath adds a keyframe for each element as it animates.
- "Then cut it for every screen, from your site to your ads." → the same video splits into 16:9, 1:1
  and 9:16, each laid out for its shape and shown in context: a browser window, a feed post, a story
  ad.
- "You always talk to the people doing the work." → the two founders, then a chat in which they
  answer a client's question.

## Phrase by phrase (October 2026)

- **"Every big project starts as one simple idea." (Bruno Morgante).** Weak (v2): his photo and a typed
  card, held for the whole line. Stronger (v3): the words build on the voice, "idea." gets a red mark,
  and the mark itself becomes the viewer's project card, which the next line tangles.
- **"…the portfolio, the plan, the PMO and the team."** Weak: four words in a row light up on one page.
  Stronger: the tangled cards line up into a ranked portfolio, the plan's bars draw with a Today line,
  every status rolls to On track, the team docks on the bars, and the project turns Delivered, each on
  its word, along an axis from Strategy to Execution.
- **"Twenty years… more than two hundred people mentored."** Weak: two numbers on a page. Stronger: the
  mark under "stick." floods the frame red, 20+ rolls like an odometer, a third zero drops in for 200+,
  and two hundred dots appear, one per person, then gather into the one organiser who gives the quote.
- **"MindMirror turns patterns from your assessment into a clearer picture."** Their own mirrored
  contour figure, drawn as tangled signal beside "why", is swept into order by a central scan line, the
  logo opening out of its own centre line: complexity to pattern, as their form asked.
- **"Not another label." (MindMirror).** Lime floods out of the person; three generic label tags fly at
  them, are struck out on "label" and fall away; the figure grows back round them as context.
- **"Great partnerships start with a clear purpose." (Amargier Advisory).** The two bars of their A mark
  slide in and lean into each other on "partnerships", like two partners; the bars return later as
  "the thinking" and "the doing", and twelve year-ticks lay the mark brick by brick for "build what
  comes next".
- **"…technology businesses across EMEA."** The mark lands on Málaga as a pin and EMEA spreads out from
  it as a dot map (Natural Earth), businesses lighting up in a wave; their links go from dashed
  (potential) to solid with an opportunity on each.

## The first intake-form videos (September 2026)

- **Headline over object, every scene (WKConversions).** Weak: a centered headline above a centered
  card in every scene, the camera locked. Stronger: vary framing and alignment by line (a close crop
  on the product for "into reality", the founders filling half the frame for "we bring it to life"),
  with the camera carrying the eye.
- **Crossfade between layouts (K.B).** Weak: the messy browser crossfades into the clean dashboard.
  Stronger: the tabs collapse into one as the camera pushes into the survivor, which unfolds into the
  dashboard: the change is a move.
- **Everything at once (K.B).** Weak: eight service cards and dozens of 16 px labels on screen for
  "integrations, websites, apps and dashboards". Stronger: one service at a time fills the frame as it
  is named, each handing over to the next, then a pull-out shows them together for a beat.

## Smooth and clean (K.B, October 2026, approved)

- **A camera move on every transition.** Weak (v2): the camera pushes 5% into each beat and releases it in 0.6 s,
  leans towards the next point on a linear segment, sways ±38 px. Karl: spikes "out of nowhere". Stronger (v3, the
  client: "great"): one slow breath for the whole film; the scenes move, the camera doesn't.
- **Cards that pop.** Weak: every card, chip and module overshoots its size and springs back. Stronger: the same
  arrivals on a smooth ease-out, no bounce.
- **A sign-off that flies around.** Weak: tool tiles orbiting the logo, pulses running along lines, a cursor.
  Stronger: the logo and the words rise in, the button follows, one slow push.
- **Hand-offs that overlap.** Weak: the next headline arrives while the last is still on screen; two layouts fade
  through each other. Stronger: each scene leaves before the next line is spoken, the way the story moves (the
  row slides out left, the axis gathers into its end point, the tool tiles gather into the app).
- **Still a bit busy (v3).** Approved, but Karl: "a bit too busy so you can't see what's happening". Measured
  (`scripts/busy_check.py`): a median of 9 separate things on screen, 8 or more for 64% of the film, together
  covering 11% of the frame; his 25 references keep a median of 4 (3–7), 8 or more for a quarter of the time,
  covering 26%. The busiest stretches (up to 19 things): the row of process chips under the hook, the dashboard
  beside the workflow card on "software, automation", the connected flow with tool chips and particles around it.
  Stronger: the same ideas with fewer, bigger things: three chips, large, instead of a row running off the frame;
  the dashboard alone and big, then the workflow card replacing it; the flow without the floating chips and
  particles; a thing leaving before the next arrives. Motion wasn't the cause: K.B moves less than most of the
  references.
