# Minimal User Interface Style

**The look.** The client's own product interface is the hero: clean, rebuilt interface cards on an
airy near-white canvas, with bold, tightly set type. It reads as a premium SaaS product film.

**Fits** software, apps, platforms and dashboards: anything with a screen. If the client has no
interface, build a truthful one around their customer's journey (booking, ordering, tracking), or
start from another direction (`styles/README.md`). Even here, a beat that isn't about the interface
gets another strategy (a number, an object, type), in the same look.

## What gives it its character

The feel to keep. The values are what the reference frames measure, there to calibrate your eye: the
brand's color and type replace them, and nothing here is a spec (`styles/README.md`, "Not literal").

- **Canvas.** Near-white with a faint cool tint (about `#FCFDFE`), never flat grey. Depth comes from
  large, soft halo circles in a pale tint of the accent (about 20–25% accent on white) sitting behind
  the interface; one thin line circle (deep navy about `#212A48`, or a pale accent line) may arc
  behind it. No textures, no photo backgrounds, no dark mode.
- **Type.** Headlines in a heavy neo-grotesk (Inter Display / Inter Tight 700–800 class, or the
  client's own sans), tightly tracked (about −0.04em) and tightly leaded, pure black, with the key
  phrase in the accent. Supporting text in regular weight, cool blue-grey (about `#76819C`). Small
  uppercase labels with wide tracking (about 0.3em). Calls to action as black pills with a white
  label and an arrow.
- **Color.** Black ink, one saturated accent (the client's primary brand color; the references use
  royal blue, about `#0457FC`) and tints of that accent. Semantic colors stay small and soft: green
  (about `#13C37B`) for positive deltas, pastel tints for tags. The accent never fills large areas.
- **Interface rendering.** White cards with large radii, very soft, wide, low-opacity shadows, and no
  borders or only hairlines. Thin monoline outline icons; bold KPI numbers; smooth line charts with a
  gradient fill; bars in graded accent tints with the key bar at full accent; status pills on tinted
  backgrounds; rounded-square icon tiles; circular avatar photos. Data is real or plausible for the
  client's business and readable, and every card keeps whitespace inside. Settled screens face the
  viewer straight-on; they may tilt slightly in depth while they fly in or while the camera pushes
  into them.
- **Imagery.** Photos only as content inside the interface or as cut-outs (avatars, product photos),
  never as backgrounds.
- **Accent mark.** A small three-stroke "spark" that punctuates a key moment, used sparingly.
- **Motion feel.** The interface does the work: numbers count up, charts draw on, bars grow, toggles
  switch, a simple black cursor clicks and drags. Cards fly in from outside the frame or rise out of
  depth and settle on strong ease-outs, secondary elements follow a few frames later, background
  layers drift slowly behind, and the camera keeps moving. No bounce; at most a small overshoot on a
  tiny pop.

## What the case decides (never taken from the references)

- **Composition and alignment of every scene**, from the line and the product: one card filling the
  frame, a close crop on a single number, a cursor journey across one screen, a full-frame headline,
  two states side by side, the product seen from an unexpected crop. The references all use one split
  layout (a text column on one side, a cluster of windows on the other). Use that at most for a single
  statement or end frame, never as the default and never in consecutive scenes.
- **Which interface appears:** only the client's real product, rebuilt from their screenshots, and only
  the part the line talks about. Charts only for real numbers; toggles, sliders and pills only where
  the product has them. No generic dashboards.
- **Everything countable:** how many cards, how they're arranged, where the type sits and how the
  headline breaks all follow the client's copy and screens.

## Never copy from the references

Each shows one generic product (calendar, shop, CRM, chat, files, AI assistant, finance, automation,
analytics). Don't reuse their products, screens, data, names, avatars, copy or headline formulas
("Turn X into Y", "Your X in Y"), and don't repeat their layouts or alignments. Use an index number
("01") only when the video really has numbered sections.
