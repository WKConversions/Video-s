# Minimal Single Visual Style

**The look.** One idea at a time, acted out by one calm, abstract form: soft, translucent, glass-like
shapes in a bright white studio, with a short statement in bold type. Quiet, premium and conceptual.

**Fits** businesses whose value is an outcome or a transformation more than a screen: services,
agencies, consultancies, platforms with abstract value, brand films, and any business without a
product interface worth showing.

**How it works with "literal meaning".** This direction is abstract by design, so start from it only
when the value is an outcome with nothing better to show (`styles/README.md`). The forms still have to
be diagrams, never vague symbols: a short statement of a few words (never the voice-over as a subtitle) states the line's claim, and the form
acts out exactly that claim so a first-time viewer reads it within a second. Growth is forms rising; convergence is many strands flowing into
one; focus is rings closing on a point; order is scattered pieces snapping into place; progress is a
path with a dot reaching the goal; simplification is overlapping shapes resolving into one. When the
line names something concrete (a number, the product, the logo), put it into the form: the number on
the destination shape, the logo as the final shape.

## What gives it its character

The feel to keep. The values are what the reference frames measure, there to calibrate your eye: the
brand's color and type replace them, and nothing here is a spec (`styles/README.md`, "Not literal").

- **Canvas.** A high-key white studio: near-white (about `#FBFBFC`) with a soft, cool haze toward the
  edges (about `#EEF2FA`) and an implied floor where forms rest, with soft blue-tinted reflections
  and contact shadows. No hard lines, textures, photos or dark mode.
- **Forms.** Few, simple, rounded primitives: squircles, rounded bars, ellipses and rings, soft blobs,
  tapering ribbons, one sphere or dot. Rendered as frosted translucent glass in pale accent tints
  (from near-white up to about `#AFD1FD`), with a soft inner gradient, a faint bright rim and overlaps
  showing through each other. The state the line arrives at is larger, single, ordered and saturated,
  with a soft accent glow; earlier states are paler, smaller, many or messy. A thin accent line with a
  solid dot may trace the path.
- **Color.** Near-white, near-black (about `#03060F`) and one accent (the client's primary brand color;
  the references use a bright blue, about `#3E7AFE`), used only for the key word, the path line and
  dot, and the glow of the arriving form. Everything else is a pale tint of it. No second hue.
- **Type.** The same heavy neo-grotesk as the other styles (or the client's own sans), short statements
  ending in a period, near-black with the key word in the accent (a faint gradient inside the word is
  in style). Supporting lines in regular weight, cool grey (about `#6D768D`). A tiny, widely tracked
  caption can carry a URL or a label.
- **Imagery.** No photos; the form carries the idea. If the film truly needs a photograph, decide it once,
  at art direction, and say so in the notes.
- **Motion feel.** Slow, smooth and weighted. Each scene plays out one transformation in real time:
  forms grow, rise, converge, snap into order or resolve into one; the line draws and the dot
  travels; the arriving form glows up. Long ease-in-outs for morphs, soft ease-outs for arrivals,
  onion-skin echoes for sweeping moves, no bounce. During holds the forms breathe, the light shifts
  and the camera keeps a very slow push or orbit, always at eye level.

## What the case decides (never taken from the references)

- **The form for each line**, from the line's claim and the client's specifics: how many shapes, which
  primitives, which direction the change runs, what the arriving form carries.
- **Composition and alignment of every scene.** The references always center a one-line headline above
  a horizontal row of forms. Use that at most for one opening or closing statement. Otherwise vary
  it: the form filling the frame, seen close or from above, off-center with the words beside it, a
  words-only moment, the camera moving through the form, a vertical progression in 9:16.
- **Transitions:** the arriving form of one scene can become the starting form of the next, or the
  camera can push through it; the ending can resolve the last form into the client's logo. Choose
  per film; don't make every scene end on a rising line with a dot.

## Never copy from the references

Their headlines all follow "From X to Y." and their sublines "One X. One Y."; don't use those
formulas. Write the client's own lines (a transformation statement is fine when the script really
makes one). Don't reuse the reference visuals (bars with a line and dot, rings around a dot, ribbons
into a square, scattered tiles into a grid, blobs separated by chevrons), their alignments or their
footer caption.
