# Visual directions

The library keeps three visual directions, each defined in words and calibrated on a contact sheet of
ten reference frames. No client picks one any more: since October 2026 the intake form doesn't ask,
and you decide every film's direction yourself. Elsewhere in the library, "the chosen style" means the
direction you decided for the film.

A direction sets the look: materials, color logic, type, rendering, imagery and motion feel. It
outranks any brand's own site look, WKConversions' included; the brand supplies the accent color, the
fonts, the logo and the wording. The case and the script decide everything else, including every
scene's layout, alignment and visual. In Karl's words: "I don't want to see the exact same alignments
and visuals. It has to suit the case and script." And on the directions themselves: "never take the
style literal, it is for your direction."

## Deciding the direction

Decide it at step 2, once the harvest has shown you the business and you have read the script, and
revisit it at art direction (step 5) only if the beats point somewhere else. Ask what a first-time
viewer has to see to believe the core message, and which world shows that best:

| The case | Start from |
|---|---|
| The value can be seen on a screen: software, apps, platforms, dashboards, a booking or ordering flow | `styles/minimal-user-interface.md`: the product's own interface does the explaining |
| The value is an outcome or a change with nothing worth showing: services, agencies, consultancies, brand films | `styles/minimal-single-visual.md`: one calm form acts out each claim |
| The story is about connecting, automating, integrating or securing: tools and data flowing into a result | `styles/minimal-gradient-saas.md`: glass tiles and cables, things flowing into a result |
| None of these: a restaurant, a shop, a physical product, a local service | a new direction in the same spirit (below) |

Then weigh what shifts the answer: the video type (a 9:16 ad needs a first frame that stops the
scroll and a denser rhythm than a website hero; a product demo needs the product), the script's tone,
and the brand's own character (a brand built on a gradient, on photography or on one bold color pulls
the look towards it). Set the tone with it: calm, confident or energetic (`motion/timing.md`), from the
script's words and the video type when nobody states one.

The answer is one direction for the whole film. You may borrow a single element from another direction
when a line needs it (a rebuilt product screen at the moment a single-visual film names the product, a
few cables in an interface film whose line is about integrations): a decision you can name in one
sentence, never a mix of looks.

**A new direction** is written down before the storyboard, in `storyboard/direction.md` next to the
thesis and in the same shape as the three: the look in one sentence, what it fits, what gives it its
character, what the case decides. It keeps the core laws
(bright by default, few and big things, the brand's own color and type, one motion identity). If it
works for more than one film, propose it for the library (the learning loop in SKILL.md).

Record the decision in the project direction (DIRECTION AND BRAND, `production/output-format.md`): the
direction, why it fits this business and script, what you changed or borrowed, and the tone. Karl sees
it with the storyboard and can redirect it there, so don't wait for him to decide it.

## Not literal

A direction's definition describes a feel and the reasons it works. Its numbers (hex values, tracking,
radii, the size of a halo) are what the reference frames measure, written down to calibrate your eye,
not a spec to rebuild:
- the brand's main color replaces the references' accent, and every tint and gradient is built from it;
- the brand's typeface replaces the suggested face when it is a clean sans (`design/typography.md`);
- shapes, radii, depth and the amount of glass, glow or grain follow the brand and the case, within the
  feel;
- the motion feel is a tendency inside the film's motion identity (`motion/motion-identity.md`), not a
  list of moves to perform;
- the case decides every layout, alignment and visual.

Two films in the same direction should look like the same studio made them, never like the same
template. When a decision is close, choose what serves this client's message over what the definition
says.

## Loading a direction

Read only the definition you start from (and, for a borrowed element, its line in the other
definition). Then view the direction's contact sheet, `styles/<direction>/sheet.jpg`, to calibrate the
feel: it shows all ten reference frames small, enough to read the look without inviting copies. Don't
study another direction's sheet for the same film, so the looks don't mix. The single frames
(`01.jpg`–`10.jpg`) are in the online library; open one only to check a rendering detail such as a
corner radius or a glow, never to borrow a layout.

Every direction keeps the motion craft (the moving camera, motion blur, overlapping builds;
`motion/animation-grammar.md`, `motion/camera.md`); the direction sets its tempo.

## Copy check

Before the storyboard goes to Karl, compare every frame with the direction's contact sheet. If a frame
shares a reference's composition or alignment, object, copy or data, redesign it until only the look
is shared.
