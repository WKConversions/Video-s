# Minimal Gradient SaaS Style

**The look.** Bright, modern SaaS energy: a soft, grainy, multi-hue gradient glowing up through a
white frame, bold type whose key phrase is filled with the same gradient, and frosted-glass tiles,
cables and app windows that show things connecting and flowing into a result.

**Fits** software and tech whose story is connecting, automating, integrating, scaling or securing:
platforms, AI tools, integrations, analytics, support tools.

## What gives it its character

The feel to keep. The values are what the reference frames measure, there to calibrate your eye: the
brand's color and type replace them, and nothing here is a spec (`styles/README.md`, "Not literal").

- **Canvas.** Near-white where the type sits, flowing into a blurred mesh gradient that is most
  saturated in the corners and pale along the edges, under a light film grain. The palette is a
  family of two or three neighboring hues built from the client's brand color (the brand color plus
  its neighbors on the color wheel); the references mostly use blue, violet and cyan (indigo-blue
  about `#4A64F4`, violet about `#6D5CFC`, pale sky and lilac about `#9CC6FC` and `#AAA1FD`), with
  variants in pink and violet, teal and blue, or orange, pink and violet. Never a flat fill, never
  hard edges in the background, no dark mode.
- **Type.** The same heavy neo-grotesk as the other styles (or the client's own sans), near-black, with
  the key phrase filled with a horizontal gradient of the palette hues. Small labels in widely
  tracked uppercase (about 0.4em), muted lavender-grey (about `#8E8FC8`).
- **Glass.** Tiles are rounded squares (corner radius about a fifth of the tile) in white frosted glass
  with a soft white rim, a very soft shadow and a colored glow underneath. App windows are
  translucent, with traffic-light dots, skeleton lines wherever detail isn't needed and real text only
  for the words that matter, and they may sit in gentle perspective so they float. Icons are
  muted-indigo monoline glyphs, with a gradient-filled glyph for the most important element.
- **Cables.** Thin curved connector lines in pale white or lilac that pick up the palette gradient
  where they matter, small glowing dots where they meet a tile, and soft orbs that can travel along
  them.
- **Depth.** 2.5D: layers at slightly different depths, gentle perspective, glows and soft shadows,
  grain over everything. More depth than the UI style, still calm; no heavy 3D, no dramatic angles.
- **Imagery.** Photos only inside app windows or tiles, never as the background.
- **Motion feel.** Flow. Cables draw in a quick stagger, pulses travel along them, the element they
  reach glows up, content builds (checks tick in order, a chart line draws to a glowing end dot,
  numbers count up, a reply appears). Tiles arrive with a soft scale-and-fade, the style's signature
  for tiles, and drift slightly at different speeds; they never bob in place. The scene's primary
  element still gets its own entrance, and consecutive scenes don't open the same way
  (`motion/animation-grammar.md`). The gradient drifts slowly while the grain stays fixed,
  and its hue balance can shift between scenes to mark chapters. A gradient may sweep through a
  headline phrase once as it settles, never on a loop. Smooth ease-in-outs for travel, soft ease-outs
  for arrivals, no bounce.

## What the case decides (never taken from the references)

- **The structure of each scene**, from what the client's product actually does. Nearly every reference
  uses the same diagram (inputs on one side, cables into a central hub, one cable into an app window,
  under a centered headline). Use a flow diagram only for a line that is genuinely about connecting or
  combining, never as the default and never in consecutive scenes. Other structures: one window
  filling the frame, a sequence of steps, hub and spokes, before and after, a close-up on a single tile
  or number, a words-only moment over the gradient.
- **Composition and alignment:** direction, crops, scale and where the type sits change from scene to
  scene; the vertical frame (9:16) gets compositions designed for it, not squeezed ones.
- **Content:** inputs are the client's real integrations and data sources; windows show the client's
  real product; the palette comes from the client's brand.

## Never copy from the references

Their headlines lean on formulas ("Turn X into Y.", "Less X. More Y.", "Faster X. Happier Y."), and
don't reuse their diagrams, alignments, data, people or eyebrow taglines. Show third-party logos
(Slack, Gmail, Notion, Trello, Google Sheets) only for apps the client really integrates with, and
don't default to the blue-violet palette: build it from the client's brand color.
