# Script analysis

Understand what the script means before choosing any visual. This step produces the beat sheet
that every later step works from.

## Beats, not sentences

A beat is a change in meaning, not a sentence boundary. Two sentences can be one beat; one sentence
can hold several. "From AI and automation to custom software, integrations, websites, apps and
dashboards" is one beat if the point is the breadth, or several if each service needs its moment.

## Analyze every beat

Fill in, for each beat:

- **VO:** the exact words, with timing from the recording's word timings when there is one (see
  `motion/timing.md`).
- **Core meaning:** what the line claims, in one plain sentence.
- **Viewer must understand:** the single thing a first-time viewer has to get, ideally with the
  sound off.
- **Importance:** 1 carries the film, 2 supports it, 3 connects.
- **Emotional tone:** frustration, relief, confidence, curiosity, urgency, pride. The tone adjusts
  tempo and path inside the film's motion personality (`motion/motion-identity.md`).
- **Visualizable nouns:** concrete things that could appear (the product, a person, an invoice, a
  number).
- **Visualizable verbs:** changes that could be shown (grows, connects, disappears, speeds up,
  breaks, merges). Verbs usually give the stronger visual, because motion shows change.
- **Beat type:** Hook, Problem, Solution, Feature, Process, Comparison, Number/data, Proof, Abstract
  concept, Emotional statement, Product demonstration, CTA, or Transition/setup. A beat can carry
  two types; name the main one first.
- **Duration and breathing room:** how long the beat has, and whether it needs a working hold after
  it for the viewer to take it in.
- **Sets up / pays off:** which earlier or later beat it connects to (see `planning/storytelling.md`).

## Rules

- **Never convert the voice-over into animated text by default.** On-screen words are one strategy
  among many (`planning/visual-strategy.md`). Use them only when the words communicate better than
  a picture: a key phrase, a number, a name, the call to action, or the one thing a muted viewer
  must read. Most lines get no text at all. The exception is a style built on short statements
  (Minimal Single Visual Style), where a few words carry each scene's claim; even there, never the
  voice-over as a subtitle.
- **Verbs before nouns.** Show the change the line describes rather than an object it names.
  Illustrating the noun of every sentence is an AI fingerprint (`design/anti-ai-design.md`).
- **Importance sets the budget.** Importance-1 beats get the strongest idea, the most screen time and
  the biggest scale; importance-3 beats stay quick and are often carried by a transition.
- **Don't invent evidence.** When a line claims something with nothing to show (no product state,
  number or proof), flag it in the storyboard notes and suggest a change; never make up numbers,
  customers or features.
- **Use the script as written.** If a line is too long for its time or has no showable content,
  suggest the change in the notes instead of rewriting it silently.
