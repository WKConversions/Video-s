# Storytelling across the film

Script analysis looks at each beat; this module looks at the whole film.

## The arc

Most commercial films move from tension to resolution: hook, problem or premise, escalation or
contrast, the turn (the product, the mechanism), proof, and resolution into the call to action.
Common narrative roles along the way: premise, escalation, contrast, reveal, mechanism, benefit,
transformation, resolution. Map each beat to its role so the film builds instead of listing.

- **The core message is the spine.** Every beat serves it, and the final beat lands it.
- **Hook:** the opening seconds must show something worth watching, not a logo or a title card.
- **Energy curve:** plot importance and tone across the beats before designing. A film that is
  equally intense everywhere feels flat; plan where it accelerates, where it holds and where it
  hits (`motion/timing.md` for rhythm).

## Setup and payoff

Strong motion design creates anticipation:
1. establish a visual rule,
2. create an expectation,
3. alter or complete the rule,
4. reveal the meaning.

Don't reveal every answer immediately.

## Visual callbacks

Reuse an object, motif, shape or spatial relationship later in the film when it strengthens
cohesion. A callback is stronger than a new visual language for every line. One example product can
run through a film if what happens to it keeps changing; one motif used as the answer to every line
turns the film into a single trick.

## Call to action

The CTA should feel like the resolution of the film, not a template pasted at the end. Where
possible, transform an existing element into the logo, product, URL, button or final statement, and
give the final message enough hold (`motion/animation-grammar.md`, end card).
