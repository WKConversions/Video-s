# Client videos from the intake form

Clients order through a Fillout form on wkconversions.com. Karl downloads a submission and gives it
to you. The master workflow in SKILL.md applies; this module covers what is specific to the form.

## Read the submission

It may arrive as CSV, XLSX, JSON, PDF or pasted text; open it with the right tool instead of guessing
at its contents. Find the answers by their labels: business name, website, video type, core message
and script, plus anything else the export carries (notes, links, files). A download can hold several
submissions: use the one Karl names, otherwise the newest, and say which. If the script is missing,
ask. For anything else, take the most reasonable reading and note it for Karl.

The form no longer asks the client for a style (October 2026), so nothing is missing when there is
none: the look is yours to decide. An older submission that still has a style answer tells you
something about the client's taste; weigh it with everything else, and never let it make the decision
for you.

## Harvest the website

Collect the logo, brand values, product visuals, copy and tools, and learn what the client sells and
to whom (`design/asset-strategy.md`, harvesting). The brand fills the direction you decide: its main
color becomes the accent, its font becomes the headline face if it's a clean sans, its logo closes the
video. The business name is the fallback wordmark. Karl may rule out parts of a client's site (for the
K.B film he ruled out their hero video); check `examples/corrections.md`.

## Decide the direction

Once the harvest has shown you the business, decide the film's look and tone yourself, as an art
director would before the kickoff: what the business sells and where its value can be seen, the video
type and where it plays, what the core message has to prove, and the brand's own character. The
library's three directions are where to start; a direction is direction, never a template, and none of
them is copied literally (`styles/README.md`). Write the decision and why into the project direction;
Karl sees it with the storyboard, so you don't stop to ask him first.

## Format and timing

The form has no length or aspect-ratio field, so derive both from the video type and say what you
chose: ads and social video, 9:16 at 15–30 seconds; website hero or explainer, 16:9 at 30–60
seconds; product demo, 16:9 at 45–90 seconds; anything else, 16:9 at the script's natural length.
With a voice-over recording, time everything to it. Without one, time the script at about 2.5 words
per second and show that timing in the storyboard, so a voice-over can be recorded to it.

## The approval stop

Deliver the storyboard as one page (`production/output-format.md`), then stop and wait for Karl's
go-ahead. In the same message, ask for the music track (or whether music goes on in the edit) and
whether a real voice-over will be recorded, so the build is timed to both. Build only after
approval; revisions go through the same code.

## Project brief (work that doesn't come through the form)

PROJECT / CLIENT-BRAND / DELIVERABLE / PLATFORM / ASPECT RATIO / RESOLUTION / FRAME RATE / TARGET
DURATION / AUDIENCE / PRIMARY OBJECTIVE / DESIRED VIEWER ACTION / SCRIPT / VOICE-OVER / BRAND ASSETS /
PRODUCT ASSETS / REFERENCES / MUST INCLUDE / MUST AVOID / TONE / DEADLINE / TECHNICAL CONSTRAINTS /
OTHER NOTES

When Karl names a look for a film, that is the direction (still taken as direction, never literally).
A client's own wish in a brief ("like our site", a reference film) is an input to the direction you
decide, the same way (`styles/README.md`).
